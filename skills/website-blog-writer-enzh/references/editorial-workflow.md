# Evidence, bilingual editorial and review

## Brief and accessible background

Build a compact brief: intended reader, problem, promise, purpose, search-led/practitioner-led/experiment-led mode when useful, scope, evidence, permitted first-party material, exclusions, structure, useful links and length. Reuse accessible approved material and explain its role briefly. Access alone is not disclosure permission. Do not treat an unpublished experiment as a successful outcome or copy a private case into the draft.

Research only what the central claim needs. A source log should identify origin, date/currentness, exact claim supported, limitations, incentives and permitted use. Prefer primary sources suited to the claim; institutional status alone does not prove currency or fit. Distinguish independent support from vendor promotion. Never fabricate citations or claim current research when none occurred. No external or paid provider is a startup dependency. If separately authorized paid research was already completed, preserve its results and cost record; a downstream prose failure is no reason to repeat it.

First-party evidence is optional for a search-led explainer, often useful for practitioner judgment and normally central to an experiment report. Do not add anecdotes to satisfy a quota. Record claim categories where helpful:

| Category | Treatment |
|---|---|
| USER CONFIRMED | Preserve the exact supported scope; truth and permission to disclose are separate. |
| PERMITTED OBSERVATION | State what was actually observed, with its limitations. |
| EXTERNAL SUPPORT REQUIRED | Cite suitable accessible evidence or qualify/remove. |
| DO NOT CLAIM | Exclude from article and derivative assets. |

Mark interpretations and hypotheses. Do not imply causality, customer results, professional authority, rankings or conversion outcomes without evidence. If a central fact is missing or disclosure is unresolved, ask the narrow question or return a blocked draft. Anonymization is insufficient when the details remain identifying.

Preventive attribution check: do not assign an omitted speaker, count, date or channel to vague feedback, or invent a "spec sheet"/"customer feedback" attribution. Keep it unattributed or out of the article. Retain both sides of conflicting figures as UNKNOWN; do not average them, turn them into a range or choose silently. With no evidence for a central health/safety/effect claim, return a blocked draft rather than fill the gap with generic assertions.

## English editorial

Follow the brief and reader's actual need. Use clear headings and direct answers where helpful. Add a Quick Answer, FAQ, table or takeaways only if it serves real reader questions; none is mandatory. Use semantic lists for discrete parallel items and ordered lists for meaningful sequence. Do not manufacture steps to justify a HowTo label.

Default to light humanization: preserve terminology, specificity and professional voice while checking whole-piece density of generic transitions, symmetry, rule-of-three repetition, forced contrasts, manufactured conclusions, marketing filler, uniform rhythm and fake conversational language. A phrase is not automatically prohibited. Do not add errors, slang, fake anecdotes or personal claims to sound human.

Review brief compliance, evidence/claim scope and naturalness separately. Low isolated stylistic findings do not require regeneration. A medium pattern may justify targeted repair; repeated high-density patterns may need broader revision without changing facts or strategy. Do not optimize toward an automated style score.

## Native Simplified Chinese

The reviewed English candidate controls facts, thesis, reasoning, examples, attribution, ownership, claim confidence and exclusions. Chinese controls syntax, terminology, paragraph rhythm, transitions and native editorial expression. Do not translate sentence by sentence.

Preserve every number, date, condition, location, limitation, link intent and evidence boundary. Use natural Chinese with an English term at first use when useful for an unfamiliar professional concept; do not annotate common abbreviations mechanically. Do not present a working label as a recognized standard. Chinese SEO metadata is editorial/semantic unless dedicated Chinese keyword validation actually occurred.

Choose lists and headings independently for Chinese readability. Check English word order, nominalization, imported metaphors/jargon, unnatural symmetry, forced triplets, empty elevation and fragmented rhythm. Compare EN/ZH facts and strength of claims explicitly, including conclusion and first-person ownership. A fluent translation that strengthens a claim fails.

## Integrity and bounded repairs

Identify a stable article key and candidate version. For file-based handoffs, record exact file paths within the chosen output location and SHA-256 for the EN candidate used by ZH, plus ZH identity. Do not call these owner-approved or frozen. If a candidate changes, invalidate and recheck only dependent work; retain the prior review record when relevant.

Compute candidate SHA-256 from the actual saved file bytes after writing, not a pre-write string or newline-normalized text. Record the same file version that the adaptation and handoff actually use; a semantically equivalent LF/CRLF variant is not the same byte identity.

For material blockers, use targeted repairs with a maximum of two attempts per blocker and four material content mutations across an article cycle. A single mutation counts once globally while incrementing each blocker it addresses. Audits and ordinary wording preferences do not consume that budget. Recheck affected claims, brief, privacy and bilingual equivalence after each mutation. Stop on repeated failure, oscillating repairs, exhausted budget or unresolved central evidence; report the narrow issue. User-requested revisions create a new review version and are not automatic repair attempts.

Do not restart completed research because of an editorial or handoff failure. Stop polishing after material checks pass and the requested deliverables are ready for review.
